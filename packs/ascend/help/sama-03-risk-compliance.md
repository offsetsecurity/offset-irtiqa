# Domain 3.2: Cyber Security Risk Management and Compliance

Domain 3.2 is how risk is managed and how compliance is checked: the risk management process and risk appetite, regulatory compliance, mandatory industry standards, security reviews and penetration tests, and independent audits.

Part of [the SAMA handbook](sama-01-how-assessment-works.md). Previous: [Domain 3.1](sama-02-leadership-governance.md). Next: [Domain 3.3](sama-04-operations-technology.md).

The domain, subdomain and control numbers, and the subdomain names, are SAMA's. Control titles and all the wording below are ours; read SAMA's own text for the same number on rulebook.sama.gov.sa.

## 3.2.1 Cyber Security Risk Management

**What SAMA requires.** A cyber security risk management process that is defined, approved and in use, and aligned with the organisation's enterprise risk management, so that risks to the confidentiality, integrity and availability of information assets are properly managed.

### 3.2.1.1 A defined, approved and implemented process

**What it asks.** The cyber security risk management process is defined, approved and implemented.

**What to do.** Write the process, have it approved, and use it.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The approved process; risk assessments carried out with it.

### 3.2.1.2 Focused on confidentiality, integrity and availability

**What it asks.** The process focuses on protecting the confidentiality, integrity and availability of information assets.

**What to do.** Assess impact on all three in every assessment.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Risk assessments scoring each of the three.

### 3.2.1.3 Aligned with enterprise risk management

**What it asks.** The process is aligned with the organisation's existing enterprise risk management process.

**What to do.** Use the same scales, appetite and reporting as the enterprise process, or map to them.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The mapping between the two; cyber risks appearing in enterprise risk reports.

### 3.2.1.4 Documented, covering all four stages

**What it asks.** The process is documented and covers risk identification, risk analysis, risk response and risk monitoring and review.

**What to do.** Make sure the document describes each stage.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The process document.

### 3.2.1.5 Covering processes, applications and infrastructure

**What it asks.** The process covers the organisation's information assets, including business processes, business applications and infrastructure components.

**What to do.** Assess risks at all three levels, not only for systems.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Risk assessments of processes, applications and infrastructure.

### 3.2.1.6 When a risk assessment must start

**What it asks.** Risk management starts early in a project, before a critical change, when outsourcing is being considered, and when new products and technologies are launched.

**What to do.** Build these four triggers into project, change, outsourcing and product processes.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Risk assessments dated at each trigger.

### 3.2.1.7 Existing assets reassessed periodically

**What it asks.** Existing information assets are risk-assessed periodically, based on their classification or risk profile.

**What to do.** Set a reassessment frequency per classification and keep to it.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Reassessment schedule; completed reassessments.

### 3.2.1.8 The right people involved

**What it asks.** Risk activities involve business owners, IT specialists, cyber security specialists and representatives of key users.

**What to do.** Record who took part in each assessment.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Participant lists on each assessment.

### 3.2.1.9 Results reported to the risk owner

**What it asks.** Risk assessment results are reported to the relevant business owner, who is the risk owner.

**What to do.** Send each assessment to its risk owner.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Evidence each result was sent to its owner.

### 3.2.1.10 Results accepted and endorsed by the risk owner

**What it asks.** The risk owner accepts and endorses the risk assessment results.

**What to do.** Obtain the risk owner's sign-off on each assessment.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Signed-off assessments.

### 3.2.1.11 Risk appetite and tolerance defined and approved

**What it asks.** The organisation's cyber security risk appetite and risk tolerance are clearly defined and formally approved.

**What to do.** Write the appetite and tolerance statements and have them approved.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The approved risk appetite and tolerance statements.

## 3.2.2 Regulatory Compliance

**What SAMA requires.** A process to identify the cyber security implications of relevant regulations, communicate them, and comply with them.

### 3.2.2.1 A process to stay compliant with regulation

**What it asks.** A process ensures compliance with the regulatory requirements that affect cyber security across the organisation. It runs periodically and whenever new requirements take effect, involves representatives from key areas of the organisation, and leads to updates of the policy, standards and procedures where needed.

**What to do.** Keep a register of applicable regulations, review it on a schedule and when new rules arrive, and record the resulting policy changes.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Regulatory register; review records; resulting policy and procedure updates.

## 3.2.3 Compliance with (Inter)national Industry Standards

**What SAMA requires.** Compliance with the mandatory national and international industry standards.

> Applies to banks. Other member organisations are excluded from this subdomain, except that if they store, process or transmit cardholder data, or use SWIFT services, they must implement PCI DSS or the SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework as relevant.

### 3.2.3.1 PCI DSS, EMV and the SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework

**What it asks.** The organisation complies with the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), the EMV technical standard, and the SWIFT Customer Security Controls Framework. SAMA's text names the SWIFT framework's March 2017 edition.

**What to do.** Confirm which of these apply to your business, and hold current evidence of compliance for each. Check SAMA's current expectation for the SWIFT framework version.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** PCI DSS attestation; EMV compliance evidence; SWIFT attestation.

*The framework was issued in 2017, and these standards have newer versions since. The current versions are what an assessor will usually expect.*

## 3.2.4 Cyber Security Review

**What SAMA requires.** Periodic reviews of the security of information assets, to check that controls are designed and implemented securely and that their effectiveness is being monitored.

### 3.2.4.1 Critical assets reviewed periodically

**What it asks.** Cyber security reviews are carried out periodically on critical information assets.

**What to do.** Set a review schedule for critical assets and keep to it.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Review schedule; review reports.

### 3.2.4.2 Customer-facing and internet-facing services: yearly review and penetration test

**What it asks.** Services used by customers or exposed to the internet are reviewed and penetration tested every year.

**What to do.** List these services and test each one at least annually.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Annual penetration test reports for each service.

### 3.2.4.3 Reviews recorded in detail

**What it asks.** Each review is recorded, including its results, the issues found and the recommended actions.

**What to do.** Use a standard review report format.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Review reports.

### 3.2.4.4 Results reported to the business owner

**What it asks.** Review results are reported to the business owner.

**What to do.** Send each report to the owner of the asset reviewed.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Evidence each report reached its owner.

### 3.2.4.5 Follow-up reviews

**What it asks.** Follow-up reviews check that all issues found were addressed, that critical risks were treated effectively, and that agreed actions are being managed.

**What to do.** Track every finding to closure and re-check it.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Follow-up review records; the findings tracker.

## 3.2.5 Cyber Security Audits

**What SAMA requires.** Thorough, independent and regular audits of the security of information assets, carried out to generally accepted auditing standards and the SAMA framework, to give reasonable assurance that controls are well designed, implemented and monitored.

### 3.2.5.1 Independent audits to recognised standards and the SAMA framework

**What it asks.** Cyber security audits are performed independently, in line with generally accepted auditing standards and the SAMA Cyber Security Framework.

**What to do.** Use auditors who are independent of what they audit, and audit against the framework.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Audit reports stating the standards and criteria used; auditor independence.

### 3.2.5.2 Following the audit manual and the audit plan

**What it asks.** Cyber security audits are carried out according to the organisation's audit manual and audit plan.

**What to do.** Include cyber security in the audit plan, and follow the manual.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The audit plan; the audit manual; audit working papers.
