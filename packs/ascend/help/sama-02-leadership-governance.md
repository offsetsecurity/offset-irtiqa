# Domain 3.1: Cyber Security Leadership and Governance

Domain 3.1 is how cyber security is directed and controlled: the committee, the CISO and their independence, the strategy, the policy, who is responsible for what, security in projects, awareness and training.

An assessor usually starts here. Without an approved governance structure, strategy and policy, most of the rest of the framework cannot get past level 2.

Part of [the SAMA handbook](sama-01-how-assessment-works.md). Previous: [How SAMA assessment works](sama-01-how-assessment-works.md). Next: [Domain 3.2](sama-03-risk-compliance.md).

The domain, subdomain and control numbers, and the subdomain names, are SAMA's. Control titles and all the wording below are ours; read SAMA's own text for the same number on rulebook.sama.gov.sa.

## 3.1.1 Cyber Security Governance

**What SAMA requires.** A governance structure for cyber security, set up and working, and endorsed by the board. Its purpose is to direct and control how the organisation approaches cyber security.

### 3.1.1.1 A cyber security committee mandated by the board

**What it asks.** Set up a cyber security committee that the board has formally mandated.

**What to do.** Have the board pass a resolution that creates the committee and gives it its mandate.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Board resolution or minutes establishing the committee.

### 3.1.1.2 Committee chaired by an independent senior manager from a control function

**What it asks.** The committee is led by an independent senior manager who comes from a control function, such as risk or compliance.

**What to do.** Appoint the chair in writing, and record which control function they come from.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Appointment of the chair; the committee membership list.

### 3.1.1.3 Who sits on the committee

**What it asks.** The committee includes senior managers from all relevant departments (for example the COO, the CIO, the compliance officer and heads of business departments) and the CISO. Internal audit may attend, but only as an observer.

**What to do.** Name the members by role and by person in the charter, and check attendance against it.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Membership list in the charter; attendance records in the minutes.

### 3.1.1.4 An approved committee charter, meeting at least quarterly

**What it asks.** A charter for the committee, written and approved, that sets out its objectives, its roles and responsibilities, the minimum number of members needed for a meeting, and how often it meets. It must meet at least once a quarter.

**What to do.** Draft the charter, have it approved, and schedule at least four meetings a year.

**Level 3 looks like.** The charter is approved and the committee meets at least quarterly, as it says.

**Records to keep.** The approved charter; minutes of every meeting, at least four a year.

### 3.1.1.5 A cyber security function

**What it asks.** Establish a dedicated cyber security function.

**What to do.** Set the function up with its own staff and mandate, and show it on the organisation chart.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Organisation chart; the function's mandate or terms of reference.

### 3.1.1.6 Cyber security independent from IT

**What it asks.** The cyber security function is independent of the IT function. To avoid conflicts of interest, the two have separate reporting lines, separate budgets and separate staff evaluations.

**What to do.** Check all three separately: who each function reports to, which budget pays for each, and who appraises each function's staff.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Organisation chart showing both reporting lines; separate budget lines; who carries out each function's staff appraisals.

### 3.1.1.7 Reports to the CEO or to the head of a control function

**What it asks.** The cyber security function reports directly to the CEO or managing director, or to the general manager of a control function.

**What to do.** Make the reporting line explicit in the organisation chart and in the CISO's job description.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Organisation chart; the CISO's job description.

### 3.1.1.8 A full-time CISO at senior management level

**What it asks.** A full-time senior manager leads the cyber security function. SAMA calls this person the CISO, and the role sits at senior management level.

**What to do.** Appoint the CISO in writing as a full-time role, at senior management grade.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Appointment letter; job description; grade.

### 3.1.1.9 The CISO: Saudi national, qualified, and cleared by SAMA

**What it asks.** The CISO must be a Saudi national and sufficiently qualified, and the organisation must obtain SAMA's no-objection before assigning the role.

**What to do.** Before appointing, confirm nationality and qualifications, and apply to SAMA for a no-objection. Do not appoint until SAMA replies.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** SAMA's no-objection letter; the CISO's qualifications and experience on file.

### 3.1.1.10 Enough budget for cyber security

**What it asks.** The board allocates enough budget to carry out the cyber security activities the framework requires.

**What to do.** Present a cyber security budget to the board, tied to the strategy and the roadmap, and record the board's approval.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Approved cyber security budget; board minutes approving it.

## 3.1.2 Cyber Security Strategy

**What SAMA requires.** A cyber security strategy that is aligned with the organisation's strategic objectives and with the banking sector's cyber security strategy, so that security initiatives and projects move the organisation where it needs to go.

> Banks must align with the banking sector's cyber security strategy. For other member organisations, SAMA makes that alignment mandatory where it is applicable.

### 3.1.2.1 A strategy that is defined, approved, kept up to date and carried out

**What it asks.** The cyber security strategy is written, approved, maintained and actually executed.

**What to do.** Write the strategy, have it approved, review it on a schedule, and track the initiatives it sets out.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The approved strategy with version history; progress reports on its initiatives.

### 3.1.2.2 Aligned with the business, the law and the banking sector

**What it asks.** The strategy is aligned with the organisation's overall objectives, with its legal and regulatory obligations, and with the banking sector's cyber security strategy.

**What to do.** Include a section in the strategy that shows each alignment explicitly.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The strategy, showing how it maps to business objectives, regulation and the sector strategy.

### 3.1.2.3 What the strategy covers

**What it asks.** The strategy explains why cyber security matters to the organisation and what it brings, describes the future state the organisation wants to reach so it stays resilient to new threats, and sets out which initiatives and projects will get it there, and when.

**What to do.** Make sure the strategy contains all three: the case for security, the target state, and a dated roadmap of initiatives.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The strategy document and its roadmap.

## 3.1.3 Cyber Security Policy

**What SAMA requires.** A cyber security policy that is defined, approved and communicated, documenting the organisation's commitment to cyber security and its objectives.

### 3.1.3.1 A policy that is defined, approved and communicated

**What it asks.** The cyber security policy is written, approved and communicated.

**What to do.** Have the policy approved through the governance route in 3.1.4, then publish it and tell staff where it is.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The approved policy; evidence it was communicated, such as acknowledgements.

### 3.1.3.2 Reviewed on a set schedule

**What it asks.** The policy is reviewed periodically, following a defined and structured review process.

**What to do.** Write down how and how often the policy is reviewed, and follow it.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Review procedure; records of each review and what changed.

### 3.1.3.3 Where the policy fits

**What it asks.** The policy is an input to other corporate policies such as HR, finance and IT; is backed by detailed standards (for example a password standard or a firewall standard) and procedures; is based on best practice and national and international standards; and is communicated to the relevant stakeholders.

**What to do.** Cross-reference the policy from the HR, finance and IT policies, and list the supporting standards and procedures.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Other corporate policies referring to it; the list of supporting standards and procedures.

### 3.1.3.4 What the policy contains

**What it asks.** The policy includes: a definition of cyber security; the organisation's cyber security objectives and scope; a statement of the board's intent; general and specific responsibilities; references to the supporting standards and procedures; and requirements that information is classified by importance, protected in line with the risk appetite, has an owner, and is risk-assessed; that people are made aware of security and what is expected of them; that regulatory and contractual obligations are met; that breaches and suspected weaknesses are reported; and that cyber security is part of business continuity.

**What to do.** Check the policy against each of these items and add what is missing.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The policy, with each required item present.

## 3.1.4 Cyber Security Roles and Responsibilities

**What SAMA requires.** Responsibilities for putting cyber security in place, keeping it working and promoting it are defined across the whole organisation, and everyone involved understands and takes on their part.

### 3.1.4.1 The board's responsibilities

**What it asks.** The board holds ultimate responsibility for cyber security. It makes sure there is enough budget, approves the committee charter, and endorses the cyber security governance, strategy and policy after the committee has approved them.

**What to do.** Put these duties in the board's terms of reference, and take each item to the board for endorsement.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Board terms of reference; board minutes endorsing the governance, strategy and policy and approving the charter and budget.

### 3.1.4.2 The committee's responsibilities

**What it asks.** The cyber security committee monitors, reviews and communicates the risk appetite, periodically and whenever it changes materially; checks that the strategy supports the organisation's objectives; and approves, communicates, supports and monitors the governance, strategy and policy, the security programmes (such as awareness, data classification, data privacy and data leakage prevention), the risk management process, and the key risk and performance indicators.

**What to do.** Put these duties in the committee charter and plan the committee's agenda around them.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Committee charter; minutes showing each duty being carried out.

### 3.1.4.3 Senior management's responsibilities

**What it asks.** Senior management makes sure standards, processes and procedures reflect security requirements; that people accept and follow the policy, standards and procedures when they are issued or updated; and that security responsibilities are written into the job descriptions of key positions and of cyber security staff.

**What to do.** Assign each duty to a named senior manager, and update job descriptions.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Job descriptions with security duties; records of staff accepting policies.

### 3.1.4.4 The CISO's responsibilities

**What it asks.** The CISO develops and maintains the strategy, policy, architecture and risk management process; makes sure detailed standards and procedures exist and are in use; delivers risk-based solutions covering people, process and technology; develops the security team; oversees security activities across the organisation, including security monitoring (the SOC), compliance monitoring, incident investigations, threat intelligence and security reviews; carries out risk assessments of information assets; supports other functions with classification, project security requirements and reviews; runs the awareness programmes; and measures and reports key risk and performance indicators.

**What to do.** Write these into the CISO's job description and report on them to the committee.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** CISO job description; reports to the committee covering these areas.

### 3.1.4.5 Internal audit carries out cyber security audits

**What it asks.** The internal audit function is responsible for performing cyber security audits.

**What to do.** Include cyber security audits in internal audit's charter and annual plan.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Internal audit charter; the audit plan; audit reports.

### 3.1.4.6 Every member of staff follows the policy

**What it asks.** All staff are responsible for complying with the cyber security policy, standards and procedures.

**What to do.** Make compliance part of employment terms, and have staff acknowledge the policy.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Employment terms; policy acknowledgements.

## 3.1.5 Cyber Security in Project Management

**What SAMA requires.** Cyber security is part of project management and project governance, so that every project meets the organisation's security requirements.

### 3.1.5.1 Security built into the project methodology

**What it asks.** Cyber security is part of the organisation's project management methodology, so that security risks are found and dealt with inside each project.

**What to do.** Add security steps and gates to the methodology document.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The project methodology showing its security steps.

### 3.1.5.2 What the methodology makes sure of

**What it asks.** The methodology makes sure that: security objectives are part of the project's objectives; the cyber security function is involved in every phase; a risk assessment at the start decides whether existing controls cover the requirements or new ones are needed; security risks go in the project risk register and are tracked; security responsibilities are assigned; and an independent party, internal or external, reviews the project's security.

**What to do.** For each project, check these six points at the right stage and record the result.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Project risk registers with security risks; start-of-project risk assessments; independent security review reports.

## 3.1.6 Cyber Security Awareness

**What SAMA requires.** An awareness programme for staff, third parties and customers, building a culture where people make sound, risk-aware decisions that protect the organisation's information.

### 3.1.6.1 An approved awareness programme

**What it asks.** Awareness programmes are defined, approved and carried out, to raise awareness and build a positive security culture.

**What to do.** Write the programme for the year, have it approved, and deliver it.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The approved programme; records of what was delivered.

### 3.1.6.2 For staff, third parties and customers

**What it asks.** Awareness is provided to three audiences: the organisation's staff, its third parties, and its customers.

**What to do.** Plan activities for each audience separately.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** The programme showing all three audiences; delivery records for each.

### 3.1.6.3 Tailored to each audience, through several channels

**What it asks.** The programme targets security behaviours, tailored to the different audiences and delivered through multiple channels.

**What to do.** Choose content and channels per audience, such as e-learning, email, posters, the website and app messages.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Materials for each audience and channel.

### 3.1.6.4 Throughout the year

**What it asks.** Awareness activities happen periodically and throughout the year, not once.

**What to do.** Spread activities across the year in a calendar.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Activity calendar; dated delivery records.

### 3.1.6.5 The minimum content

**What it asks.** At a minimum, awareness explains the security measures in place, people's roles and responsibilities, and relevant new threats and events (for example spear-phishing and whaling).

**What to do.** Check the materials cover all three.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Awareness materials.

### 3.1.6.6 Evaluated for effectiveness

**What it asks.** The programme is evaluated to measure how effective its activities are and to recommend improvements.

**What to do.** Measure results, for example phishing simulation rates or quiz scores, and record the improvements you make.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Effectiveness measurements; the recommendations and changes made.

### 3.1.6.7 Customer awareness with suggested protections

**What it asks.** Customer awareness covers both retail and commercial customers and, at a minimum, lists security measures customers can consider to reduce their own risk.

**What to do.** Publish customer guidance for both groups, with practical steps they can take.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Customer awareness materials for retail and commercial customers.

## 3.1.7 Cyber Security Training

**What SAMA requires.** Staff are trained to operate the organisation's systems securely and to apply the security controls their roles require.

### 3.1.7.1 Specialist training by role

**What it asks.** Staff receive specialist or security-related training suited to their job, including: people in key roles; the cyber security team; people who develop and technically maintain information assets; and people involved in risk assessments.

**What to do.** Identify each group, set the training each needs, and deliver it.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Training plan by role; attendance and completion records.

### 3.1.7.2 Education to operate systems securely

**What it asks.** Staff are educated so they have the skills and knowledge to operate the organisation's information assets securely.

**What to do.** Include secure operation in induction and in role training.

**Level 3 looks like.** Defined and approved in writing, carried out in a structured way, shown with evidence, and checked for compliance.

**Records to keep.** Training content; completion records.
